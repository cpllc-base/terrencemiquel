Observation 03 Permission May 31, 2025

Life had already given me permission. I was waiting for a person to confirm it.

By Terrence Miquel · a 3-minute read
1 What I was handed

Authority, credentials, or approval have to come before meaningful action.

The assumption ran quietly underneath a lot of decisions: before you act, someone with more authority than you needs to say go. A title, a credential, a nod of approval — meaningful action waits for someone else's permission slip.

2 The test

I had the ideas, the responsibility, and the experience. I was still waiting to be chosen.

I could point to real responsibility already sitting in my hands — problems I could see clearly, value I was positioned to contribute, experience that was already mine. And some part of me was still standing at the door, waiting for a person to open it.

✓ What survived

Responsibility itself is a form of permission.

If life has placed something within your reach and made you accountable for it, you are already authorized to respond to it. The permission wasn't missing. It was just wearing a disguise I didn't recognize — it looked like responsibility, not a certificate.

The principle that held

"Sometimes the permission you're waiting for arrived disguised as responsibility."

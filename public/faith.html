I test faith against reality. Not religion.

What I hear→ The test→ Locked in

It starts with relationship — a God who still speaks and leads, not a closed book I'm left to interpret alone. That gives me two things to weigh: what I read in the text, and what I sense him prompting. Neither one lands as a conclusion. Each lands as a hypothesis — a claim about what's true and what to do.

Then reality gets a vote — and the text is my plumb line. God won't contradict his own character, so I hold every prompting against what's written, watch the fruit over time, and check it with people wiser than me. If it doesn't hold, the leading wasn't him or I read it wrong. If it holds, I lock it in.

The hard part was never whether he speaks. It's telling his voice from my own wanting — the thing I already wanted, wearing a halo. So I hold the voice to the same test as everything else. What I genuinely can't run this way — his nature, what waits after — reason and trust carry me there. How he leads, and how the principles actually live? Reality decides.

Religion's mistake isn't using the text. It's stopping there — doctrine with no living author still speaking behind it. That's the part I let go. I want the author — and I test what I hear because I believe he's really speaking, and it matters to get it right.

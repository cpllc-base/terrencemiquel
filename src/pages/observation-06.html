Observation 06 Character June 9, 2025

The rep doesn't happen during rehearsal. It happens when reality pushes back.

By Terrence Miquel · a 3-minute read
1 What I was handed

Decide who you want to be. Repeat it. Stay motivated.

The advice was everywhere, and it sounded complete: decide who you want to be, repeat it back to yourself until it sticks, and keep your motivation up so you don't quit. Say the identity out loud enough times and it becomes true.

So that's what I did — morning identity statements, said with conviction, meant to install the person I was deciding to become.

2 The test

Morning statements prepared me. They didn't prove anything.

The statements did what rehearsal does — they got me ready. But rehearsal isn't the test. The real test showed up later in the day, uninvited: tired, frustrated, tempted, rejected, or under pressure I hadn't scheduled.

That's where the gap showed up. The version of me I'd rehearsed in a quiet room in the morning wasn't automatically the version that showed up when reality pushed back in the afternoon.

✓ What survived

Rehearsal prepares the mind. Resistance trains the nervous system.

Saying the identity is a mental rep — useful, but it's not the whole rep. The full rep is deciding, then holding that decision under actual resistance: the fatigue, the friction, the moment it would've been easier to fold. That's the part rehearsal can't simulate.

Repeat that enough times and something shifts that repeating words alone never touches — the nervous system starts expecting to hold, instead of bracing to break.

The principle that held

"You can rehearse an identity in private, but it only becomes character through reps in reality."

This is close to the center of Aligned Momentum — the book I'm writing about building habits that turn a rehearsed identity into trained character.

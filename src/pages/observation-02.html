Observation 02 Direction May 28, 2025

A decision is a marker in the future. Choices reveal whether you are traveling toward it.

By Terrence Miquel · a 3-minute read
1 What I was handed

Decisions and choices are essentially the same thing.

I treated them as interchangeable — decide something once, and the choices required to get there would just fall in line behind it. Declare the destination and the moving-toward-it part would basically take care of itself.

2 The test

Declaring a destination didn't determine what happened in between.

The decision stayed fixed. What happened in the hours and days between here and there did not automatically match it. Repeated small choices kept getting made — separately, in the moment — regardless of what I'd already decided in a single moment earlier.

✓ What survived

A decision establishes where you intend to arrive. Choices determine whether you actually move.

A decision is a marker planted in the future — it names the destination. It doesn't walk there for you. Every choice afterward either moves toward that marker or drifts from it, regardless of how firmly the decision was made.

The principle that held

"A decision places the pin. Your choices create the route."
